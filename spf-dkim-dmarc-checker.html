How to run a check

Are your emails landing in spam? Your DNS records could be the reason. Click the checker in your toolbar, type your domain or an email address, and press Check. In seconds, each record gets Pass, Check or Fix, so you know exactly what needs work. Here's what good looks like.

What good SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX look like

SPF: one record, ending in ~all or -all, with ten DNS lookups or fewer. DKIM: a key is found, ideally 2048 bits. Custom selector? Add it here. DMARC: set to quarantine or reject, with a reporting address for daily reports. And MX should point to your email provider's mail servers.

What it checks

Every check, record by record

The rules come from the SPF, DKIM and DMARC RFCs and Google's email sender guidelines.

SPF RFC 7208

  • Finds your SPF record and flags more than one record
  • Counts DNS lookups against the 10-lookup limit
  • Catches syntax errors, such as a misspelled mechanism or an invalid IP range
  • Catches include: and redirect= names with no SPF record, and terms placed after all, which receivers ignore
  • Marks +all as a Fix, and warns about ?all and the outdated ptr mechanism

DKIM RFC 6376

  • Looks for keys on 28 common selectors, including the ones used by Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Mailchimp, SendGrid and Fastmail
  • Lets you enter your own selector, or paste it straight from a DKIM-Signature header
  • Reads the exact key size, and flags damaged, revoked and too-short keys

DMARC RFC 7489

  • Reads your policy: none, quarantine or reject
  • Checks that the report addresses in rua= are valid mailto: addresses
  • Explains pct= when only part of your failing mail gets the policy
  • Checks the parent domain when a subdomain has no record of its own, including its subdomain policy, sp=

MX RFC 5321

  • Lists your mail servers in priority order
  • Recognizes common providers such as Google Workspace and Microsoft 365
  • Flags a common mistake: MX points to Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, but SPF does not include it
  • Flags a null MX, which can get mail from that domain rejected

Plus the extras: it shows whether BIMI and MTA-STS records are published.

The targets

What good SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX look like

If your domain matches all four, the essentials are in place.

SPF
One record, ending in ~all or -all, with 10 DNS lookups or fewer. The checker warns at 9 or 10, because one more sending service would break SPF.
DKIM
A key is found, ideally 2048 bits. Not on a common selector? Enter yours under DKIM selector (optional).
DMARC
p=quarantine or p=reject, with a rua=mailto: address so you get daily reports. p=none only monitors.
MX
Your email provider's mail servers.

Good to know: the checker reads public DNS records. It cannot see how a specific inbox provider treats your mail, so use it to get the foundations right, then watch your DMARC reports. Guides: DMARC failing, SPF over 10 lookups and MX records.

FAQ

Questions about the checker

Is the SPF, DKIM & DMARC Checker free?

Yes. It is free, with no sign-up, no account and no paid plan.

What permissions does it need?

None. The extension asks for no browser permissions, so Chrome shows no permission warning when you add it, and it cannot read the pages you visit.

Does imisofts see the domains I check?

No. The lookups go straight from your browser to Google Public DNS over HTTPS, with Cloudflare DNS as a backup. imisofts receives no data, and your last five checks stay in your browser. The full details are in the extension privacy policy.

Why does it say no DKIM key was found?

The checker looks for keys on 28 common selectors. If your provider uses another one, find the s= value in the DKIM-Signature header of an email you sent, and enter it under DKIM selector (optional). You can also paste the whole header there.

How many DNS lookups can an SPF record use?

Ten. RFC 7208 limits SPF to 10 DNS lookups, and every include:, a, mx, ptr, exists and redirect= counts, including the ones inside your includes. Over the limit, SPF fails. The checker counts them for you. Our guide shows how to get back under 10.

What is a good DMARC policy?

p=quarantine or p=reject, with a rua=mailto: address so you get daily aggregate reports. p=none only monitors: receivers are not asked to treat failing mail any differently. See how to set up DMARC monitoring.

Can it tell me if my email will land in spam?

No tool that reads DNS can promise that. The checker reads your public DNS records, which inbox providers look at, but it cannot see how a specific inbox provider treats your mail. Correct SPF, DKIM and DMARC are the foundation the rest is built on.

Can I check a domain I do not own?

Yes. SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX records are public DNS records, so you can check any domain, for example a client's or a competitor's.
