AI Data Licensing Deals: $203M at Reddit, $75M+ at Informa

Short answer. The biggest single deal figure in the filings we read is Reddit's: in January 2024 it signed data licensing deals with an aggregate contract value of $203.0 million and terms of two to three years, according to its final IPO prospectus. Taylor & Francis earned $75m+ from AI data access deals in 2024, its parent Informa reports, and a single Wiley AI licensing agreement of $23 million lifted its quarter to April 30, 2024. Other well publicised deals we checked, including News Corp's with OpenAI, were announced with no price.

That gap matters if you own data and want to price it. Public numbers come from a handful of companies that report to investors, and in the passages we read none of them gives a price per record. Our AI data licensing service page explains what we offer.

This guide lists what each company disclosed, where it disclosed it and what the number covers. Every figure comes from a filing, an annual report or the company's own announcement, read on November 27, 2026.

Reddit's $203.0 million (a batch of contracts) and Wiley's $23 million (one agreement) are deal figures. The rest are expected revenue or revenue lines that mix several deals, and Shutterstock's line also includes products that have nothing to do with AI training. Read those as the size of a seller's data business, not as the price of one deal.

Reddit: $203 million of contracts signed in one month

Reddit disclosed these deals in its IPO prospectus, filed March 21, 2024, about two months after signing. The prospectus calls Reddit data "a foundational piece to the construction of current AI technology and many LLMs." On the contracts it says: "In January 2024, we entered into certain data licensing arrangements with an aggregate contract value of $203.0 million and terms ranging from two to three years." It expected "a minimum of $66.4 million of revenue to be recognized" in 2024.

Spread evenly, $203.0 million over two to three years works out to about $68 million to $102 million a year across all of those buyers. That is our arithmetic, not a figure Reddit gave, and the passages we read do not name the buyers or split the total.

The filing also explains how such deals are built. Reddit says it provides "customers with the right to access data from our platform over the contractual period", and that the price "is generally a fixed fee or usage-based fee." Its 10-Q for the third quarter of 2024 adds that it recognizes this revenue "generally ratably over the license period", so the money is booked over the term rather than at signing.

Reddit's later reports fold licensing into "other revenue", which its 10-K for 2025 describes as "revenue from content licensing and products sold directly to users." That line grew from $15.2 million in 2023 to $114.7 million in 2024 and $140.0 million in 2025. Even in 2025 it was about 6% of Reddit's $2.2 billion in revenue.

Academic publishers: Wiley and Taylor & Francis

Two academic publishers disclosed AI income in their results. Informa, the owner of Taylor & Francis, wrote in its 2024 annual report: "Our AI partnerships generated $75m+ of non-recurring data access revenue for Taylor & Francis in 2024." Note the word non-recurring: Informa calls these "one-off data access agreements".

Wiley disclosed a single deal by its effect on results. Its earnings release for the year ended April 30, 2025 says fourth quarter Learning revenue was down "due to a $23 million AI licensing agreement in the prior year", meaning the quarter ended April 30, 2024. A year later, its fiscal 2026 release said "AI revenue grew double digits to nearly $50 million with a rapidly expanding recurring stream", naming partnerships with IQVIA and OpenEvidence.

Wiley's shift to recurring AI revenue is worth watching. In our view, a one-time archive license pays once, while a recurring license can pay again as the buyer keeps using fresh data. Our guide to the terms of an AI data licensing agreement covers how term, renewal and exclusivity clauses change what you earn.

Stock media: Shutterstock's data line

Shutterstock reports a revenue line called Data, Distribution, and Services. Its 10-K filings for 2024 and 2025 say these products "include, among other things, the use of our metadata, leveraging our Giphy, Inc. platform, and customized Shutterstock Studios offerings", and that it has "seen increased demand for access to our metadata for machine learning and generative artificial intelligence model training."

The line grew about 5.3 times from 2022 to 2025. Because it also holds Giphy and Studios, the filing does not tell you how much of it is AI training data alone. On January 6, 2025, Shutterstock agreed to combine with Getty Images in a merger of equals, according to its 10-K for 2024.

Big deals announced with no price

Several of the best known deals were announced without a number. These are the primary announcements we read:

  • Google and Reddit, February 22, 2024. Google's own post says it "now has access to Reddit's Data API" to "display, train on, and otherwise use" Reddit content. No price.
  • News Corp and OpenAI, May 22, 2024. OpenAI's announcement describes "a historic, multi-year agreement" giving access to "current and archived content" from titles including The Wall Street Journal, The Times and The Australian. No price.
  • Dotdash Meredith and OpenAI, 2024. IAC's first quarter 2024 release says Dotdash Meredith's content "will be used to improve OpenAI's large language models" and that attributed content will appear in ChatGPT responses. No price in that release.

Press reports have put figures on some of these deals. We did not find those figures in the companies' own documents, so we do not repeat them here.

What the filings tell a company that owns data

Four patterns stand out in the documents above. They are our reading of the filings, not rules.

  • The biggest total came from a huge archive. Reddit's $203.0 million rests on what its prospectus calls a "massive corpus of conversational data". The publishers' totals are smaller.
  • Reddit's terms are two to three years. In our view, a term that ends is also a chance to reprice at renewal.
  • Pricing is fixed or usage based. Reddit's words. Access over a period, not a sale of the data outright.
  • Some of the money is one-off. Informa calls its 2024 deals non-recurring. Wiley now stresses recurring AI revenue.

In the passages we read, none of these sellers published a price per record, per token or per file, so there is no public rate card to copy. Nothing in these filings shows what a smaller data set would fetch. For the buyers on the other side of these deals, see who buys AI training data.
