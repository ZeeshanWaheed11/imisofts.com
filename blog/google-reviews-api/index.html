Short answer. There is no single "Google Reviews API". Google offers two official routes, and which one you can use depends on whose reviews you want. For reviews of a business you own or manage, the Google Business Profile API returns every review of a verified location, 50 per page, and Google lists no fee for it; you need a profile verified and active for 60+ days, a website, and an approved access request. For any other business, the Places API returns at most five reviews per place, billed as Place Details Enterprise + Atmosphere at $25 per 1,000 calls after 1,000 free calls a month. That is $5 per 1,000 reviews in the first price tier if every place returns five, and Google's terms say you may not copy and save user reviews. Scrapers such as Apify's Google Maps Reviews Scraper, which Apify says extracts all reviews, charge $0.30 to $0.60 per 1,000 reviews plus a plan fee of $0 to $999 a month, but Google's Maps terms bar copying and bulk downloading its content. Which Google reviews API answers which question

Google splits reviews by ownership. The Business Profile API (still called the Google My Business API in parts of its docs) is for the owner or an authorized manager of a listing. It can list, read, reply to and delete replies on reviews. The Places API is for showing information about any place inside your own app, and it returns a sample of reviews, not the full history.

  • Your own reviews, or a client's: Business Profile API. Full history, replies, no listed fee, approval needed.
  • A competitor's or a prospect's rating: Places API. Rating and review count on the Enterprise SKU, up to five review texts on Enterprise + Atmosphere.
  • Every review of a business you do not manage: we found no Google page offering this; reviews.list needs a verified location and the business.manage permission. That is the gap scrapers fill, and it is where the terms risk sits.

Business Profile API: all your reviews, no listed fee

Google's reference for accounts.locations.reviews.list says it "Returns the paginated list of reviews for the specified location" and that it "is only valid if the specified location is verified." Each page holds up to 50 reviews ("The maximum pageSize is 50"), and you can sort by rating or by update time. The response also carries the location's average rating and total review count. A separate method, batchGetReviews, returns reviews from several locations in one request, which suits an agency or a multi-location business.

Getting in is the hard part. Google's prerequisites page says every applicant must "Manage a Google Business Profile that is verified and active for 60+ days" and "Have a website representing the business listed on the GBP." The profile can be your own office or one that belongs to a client you manage, and you apply with an email address listed as an owner or manager on that profile. Google's FAQ says requests "are reviewed within 14 days." Google's prerequisites page says you can tell from your project's quota: 0 queries per minute means not yet approved, 300 means approved.

We found no fee for the Business Profile APIs on Google's review, prerequisites, quota or FAQ pages, so we describe it as no listed fee rather than free forever. At 300 requests a minute and 50 reviews a page, an approved project could in theory pull up to 15,000 reviews a minute, our arithmetic on Google's two numbers.

Two lines in the Business Profile API policies matter for anyone building a reviews tool. You "must not automate or trigger review replies" without "the user's prior specific and express consent", so an AI that answers reviews needs the owner's consent first. And content may not be stored "for use outside of your Business Profile project" except in limited amounts, kept "for no more than 30 calendar days", securely, and it "cannot be manipulated or aggregated in any way."

Places API: five reviews per place, $25 per 1,000 calls

Place Details (New) is the call that returns reviews for any business with a place ID. Google's Place Details guide is precise about which fields cost what. Asking for rating and userRatingCount "triggers the Place Details Enterprise SKU". Asking for reviews and reviewSummary returns "up to five user-submitted Review objects and an AI-generated summary of reviews" and "triggers the Place Details Enterprise + Atmosphere SKU".

So 2,000 review calls in a month cost $25 (the first 1,000 are free), and 10,000 cost $225. If every place returns all five reviews, $25 buys 5,000 reviews, or $5 per 1,000 reviews in the first tier. A place with fewer than five reviews returns fewer, which raises the cost per review. Google bills usage monthly by volume range, so the price per 1,000 calls falls with volume: $20 above 100,000 calls, $15 above 500,000, $7.50 above 1 million and $2.28 above 5 million, which is about $0.46 per 1,000 reviews on those calls, close to a scraper's rate. If you only need a star rating to qualify a lead, Google itself suggests leaving out the reviews field so the call stays on the cheaper Enterprise SKU.

The Places API also comes with display duties. Google's policy page says "You must always credit the author when displaying photos or reviews", and users must be able to open each review on Google Maps through its googleMapsUri. Our Google Places API pricing guide covers the rest of the Places price list.

Scrapers: every review, $0.30 to $0.60 per 1,000

For all reviews of a business you do not manage, the usual tool is a scraper. Apify's Google Maps Reviews Scraper, maintained by Apify, says it extracts "all reviews of Google Maps places using place URLs", with review text, date, owner response, review URL and reviewer details. Its pricing tab, read 26 November 2026:

  • Free plan, $0 a month: $0.60 per 1,000 reviews, with "$5 to spend", or "up to 8,333 scraped reviews".
  • Starter, $19 a month: $0.45 per 1,000.
  • Scale, $199 a month: $0.40 per 1,000.
  • Business, $999 a month: $0.30 per 1,000.

Platform usage is included, plus $0.00005 per actor start. Apify's page lists each paid plan's fee as the same amount "to spend" (for example "$19 to spend" on Starter), so the plan fee is the floor of your bill: 10,000 reviews on Starter cost $19 in total ($1.90 per 1,000 effective), and 50,000 reviews come to $22.50. You can try the Google Maps Reviews Scraper on Apify's free plan before choosing a plan.

Cost per 1,000 reviews, side by side

On price alone, a scraper is usually cheaper per review than the Places API at small and medium volume: $0.45 per 1,000 on Apify Starter (plus the $19 plan, so $19 for 10,000 reviews) against $5 per 1,000 reviews in Google's first tier ($25 for 10,000 reviews from 2,000 calls, if each place returns five). Above 5 million calls a month Google's rate falls to about $0.46 per 1,000 reviews. But the cheapest route of all is the one that pays us nothing. If the reviews are your own or a client's, the Business Profile API has no listed fee, returns the full history, and lets you reply. Paying a scraper to read reviews you could pull through Google's own API is money you do not need to spend.

What Google's terms allow you to keep

This is where the price comparison stops being the whole story. Google's Places API policies say "Don't export, extract, or scrape Google Maps Content for use outside the Services", and give as an example "copy and save business names, addresses, or user reviews." Google does let you store "exempt identifiers such as the place ID" so you can "fetch fresh details when a user interacts with a place."

The Google Maps Additional Terms of Service say you may not "copy the content", "mass download or create bulk feeds of the content (or let anyone else do so)", or use Google Maps "to create or augment any other mapping-related dataset (including ... business listings database, mailing list, or telemarketing list)". In our view, a reviews scraper fits the bulk copying those terms describe. Whether that is lawful where you are is a separate question from whether it breaks Google's terms; our guide on whether web scraping is legal covers the legal question. This is general information, not legal advice.

In our view, the low-risk uses are reading your own reviews through the Business Profile API and showing a live rating or up to five reviews with attribution through the Places API. Google's terms make no exception for scraped reviews kept for internal use.

Which route to pick

  • A business owner or agency tracking its own reviews: Business Profile API. Apply early: you need a profile verified and active for 60+ days, and Google says requests are reviewed within 14 days.
  • A lead list scored by rating: Places API Enterprise SKU for rating and review count only, at $20 per 1,000 after 1,000 free. Our Google Maps scraper comparison covers the listings side.
  • Competitor review analysis at volume: we found no official route that returns every review; a scraper does, at $0.45 per 1,000 on Apify Starter plus the plan fee, with the terms risk above.
