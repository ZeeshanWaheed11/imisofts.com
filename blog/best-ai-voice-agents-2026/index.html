Every AI voice agent platform advertises a per minute rate. On four of the six platforms below, that rate is not what you pay. It covers one layer of the stack, and the language model, the voice, and the phone call are billed separately on top.

The short answer. If you want the clearest bill, use Retell AI, which publishes every component. If you want full control of the stack, use Vapi. If you want one number that covers the AI, use Bland, but check the feature table first because warm transfer is not on the self serve plans. If you care most about how the voice sounds, use ElevenLabs Agents. If you are an enterprise, Synthflow now starts at 30,000 dollars a year. And if you already run your business or your clients on GoHighLevel, GoHighLevel Voice AI is the cheapest all in option at normal small business volume, because the AI layer is flat rather than metered.

The number on the pricing page is not the price

A voice agent needs four things on every call: speech to text so it can hear, a language model so it can think, text to speech so it can talk, and a phone line. Platforms differ in how many of those four they include in the headline rate.

Vapi publishes 0.05 dollars per minute. Its own pricing page labels that "Vapi Hosting Cost" and says plainly that it "excludes model provider costs", which are passed through at cost. Retell publishes a band of 0.07 to 0.31 dollars per minute and then, unusually, prints the whole component table so you can see why the band is that wide. Bland's rate covers the model, the speech to text and the voice in one number, with telephony separate.

Bland makes the point about its rivals directly on its own pricing page, so treat it as a competitor's framing rather than neutral analysis: it says the advertised rates at Vapi and Retell are "the platform fee only" and estimates real production stacks land near 0.13 to 0.30 dollars per minute on Vapi and 0.11 to 0.25 on Retell. We could not verify Vapi's end of that, because Vapi does not publish provider rates. We could check Retell's, and the arithmetic holds.

Retell AI: the most transparent pricing in the category

Retell is the platform we would hand to a business that wants to understand its own invoice. It starts at zero with 10 dollars of free credits, includes 20 concurrent calls at no charge, and publishes a rate for every layer: Retell voice infrastructure at 0.055 dollars per minute, text to speech at 0.015 (or 0.040 if you pick ElevenLabs voices), the language model from 0.003 per minute on a nano model up to 0.16 on the largest, and US telephony at 0.015.

Three details from Retell's own FAQ are worth more than any feature list. Calls are tracked to the nearest second with no per call rounding. You are not billed for calls that fail to connect. And you are billed during silence and hold, because the transcription engine keeps listening. That last one is the line item that surprises people, and almost no roundup mentions it.

The catch: SOC 2 is in place, but HIPAA, SSO, role based access and a dedicated server are all Enterprise. Add ons stack up quickly too, at 0.005 per minute for a knowledge base, 0.01 for PII removal and 0.10 per outbound branded call.

Vapi: control, if you have a developer

Vapi is the developer platform of the group, used by Amazon Ring, Intuit, ServiceTitan and New York Life, and it raised a 50 million dollar Series B. You pay 0.05 dollars per minute for hosting and bring your own model providers. If you supply your own API keys, the provider cost through Vapi is zero and you pay those vendors directly.

That is genuinely the cheapest structure at scale, and it is the only one where you can swap a model without changing platforms. It is also the one where you cannot know your cost per minute from the pricing page alone.

The catch: concurrency is 10 lines included and 10 dollars per line per month after that, call history is kept 14 days, and there is no SSO, no role based access and no SOC 2 on the self serve Build plan. HIPAA is a 2,000 dollar per month add on and Zero Data Retention is 1,000 dollars per month, on either plan.

Bland AI: one flat rate, one large catch

Bland's pitch is a single number. Start is 0.14 dollars per minute with no platform fee and no card required, Build is 0.12 with a 299 dollar monthly fee, Scale is 0.11 with a 499 dollar fee. The model, the transcription and the voice are all inside that rate, with no token charges. Telephony is separate, and if you bring your own carrier you pay no transfer fees at all. Compliance is the strongest here on paper: SOC 2 Type I and II, HIPAA with a signed BAA, GDPR and PCI DSS, with a 99.9 percent uptime SLA on every tier including the free one.

Two things buyers get wrong. First, the platform fees are traps at low volume. Run the break even: the 299 dollar Build plan only beats Start past 14,950 talk minutes a month, and Scale only beats Build past 20,000. Below that, the cheapest Bland plan is the one with no fee.

The catch, and it is a big one: on Bland's own comparison table, warm transfers, live transfers, guardrails, SMS and web chat, and the appointment scheduling node are all marked as Enterprise only. An AI receptionist that cannot warm transfer to a human is a different product from the one most buyers think they are purchasing.

ElevenLabs Agents: the best voices, and a tier ladder that gives you nothing

ElevenLabs makes the voices half this industry resells, so if your objection to AI calls is that they sound like AI, start here. There is a free tier with 15 minutes, then Starter at 6 dollars, Creator at 22, Pro at 99, Scale at 299 and Business at 990 dollars a month.

Now do the division. Starter buys 75 minutes, Creator 275, Pro 1,238, Scale 3,738, Business 12,375. Every single one of those works out to exactly 0.080 dollars per minute, and additional minutes past your allowance are also 0.080. In other words, climbing the ladder from 6 dollars to 990 dollars a month buys you concurrency and workspace seats, and not one cent of volume discount. Going over your concurrency limit triggers burst pricing at 0.160 per minute, which is double.

The catch: the language model and the telephony are external and billed at cost on every tier, so 0.080 is a floor and not a price.

Synthflow: it left the small business market

Worth flagging because a lot of 2026 roundups still list Synthflow's old self serve tiers. Its pricing page today has exactly one plan on it: "Enterprise contracts start at 30,000 dollars annually", scoped around call volume, telephony, integrations and security review. That is 2,500 dollars a month before you place a call.

For the right buyer it is credible, with 65 million voice calls a month across 30 plus countries, SOC 2, HIPAA, ISO 27001, GDPR and a choice of EU or US hosting. For a plumber who wants his phone answered, it is no longer in the running.

GoHighLevel Voice AI: the pick if you already run GoHighLevel

GoHighLevel is not a specialist voice platform, and we would not recommend buying it only for the voice agent. But if you already run your agency or your clients on it, the maths is hard to argue with, because it is the only option here where the AI layer is flat instead of metered.

AI Employee Unlimited is 97 dollars a month per enabled location and includes unlimited Voice AI across inbound, outbound and the web widget, subject to fair use. AI Employee Growth is 50 dollars and includes 100 Voice AI minutes covering all three modes combined. Below those, pay per use bills three components: Voice Engine at 0.045 dollars per minute since May 20 2026, text to speech, and model tokens. Phone charges always apply on top, on every plan, which HighLevel states explicitly.

The single biggest cost lever is the voice you choose. HighLevel's own table prices OpenAI and Cartesia text to speech at 0.015 dollars per minute and ElevenLabs V3 at 0.170, which is 11 times more expensive for the same minute. On 1,000 minutes that one dropdown is a 155 dollar swing. Pick the voice deliberately.

The catch, and agencies must read this one: 97 dollars is per location. Ten sub accounts is 970 dollars a month, not 97. Rebilling AI usage to your clients requires the 497 dollar a month agency plan. Agent Studio is pay per use on every plan, included in none. And "unlimited" is subject to a fair use policy under which HighLevel may throttle, limit or terminate.

What 1,000 minutes a month actually costs

Same workload on every platform: 1,000 inbound talk minutes a month, a mid tier language model, a standard rather than premium voice, and US phone charges of roughly 0.015 dollars per minute. Every figure comes from the vendor's published rates on July 31 2026.

  • GoHighLevel AI Employee Unlimited: about 112 dollars (97 flat plus phone charges), and the effective rate keeps falling as volume rises
  • Retell AI: about 132 dollars (0.055 infrastructure plus 0.015 voice plus 0.045 model plus 0.015 telephony, plus 2 dollars for the number)
  • Bland Start: about 155 dollars (0.14 all in for the AI, plus telephony, no platform fee)
  • ElevenLabs Agents Pro: about 159 dollars (99 for 1,238 minutes, plus the model and the phone line on top)
  • Bland Build: about 434 dollars, because the 299 dollar platform fee has nothing to amortise at this volume
  • Vapi: not computable from the pricing page. Hosting is 50 dollars, and the rest depends on the providers you choose
  • Synthflow: not available. The floor is 2,500 dollars a month

The ordering changes completely at 20,000 minutes a month, where Bland Scale and a tuned Vapi stack pull ahead and the flat plans stop being remarkable. Price your own volume, not ours.

What none of these platforms fix for you

Every vendor on this page sells the agent. None of them sell the parts that actually decide whether the project works.

Consent is your problem. On February 8 2024 the FCC adopted a declaratory ruling confirming that an AI generated voice counts as an "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the TCPA, so calls using one require the called party's prior express consent. Buying a platform does not give you that consent, and none of these tools will check it for you. Talk to a lawyer before you dial a single consumer, and if you want the consent, DNC and opt-out plumbing built properly, that is our TCPA compliant AI calling service.

The handoff decides everything. The most important question is not how human the agent sounds, it is what happens in the fifteen seconds after it fails. Confirm the transfer path exists on the plan you are actually buying, which is exactly where Bland's self serve tiers fall down.

Silence is billable. Retell says so outright, and the same physics applies everywhere. Hold music, a caller rummaging for a policy number, a long pause: all of it meters.

A 30 day pilot that tells you the truth

Pick one call type, not "the phones". After hours missed calls is the best first pilot in almost every business, because the alternative is a voicemail nobody returns.

Then measure four things: cost per completed call rather than cost per minute, the transfer rate to a human, the booking or qualification rate against your current baseline, and the abandon rate in the first 20 seconds. Run it for 30 days on a pay as you go plan with no annual commitment, which rules out Synthflow and any contract vendor for a pilot.

The bottom line

Pick by the job, not by the leaderboard. Retell for a clear bill and a fast start. Vapi if you have a developer and want to own the stack. Bland if flat rate simplicity matters more than the self serve feature gaps. ElevenLabs if the voice is the product. Synthflow if you have an enterprise budget and a security review. And GoHighLevel if you are already on it, where 97 dollars a month per location for unlimited voice minutes is the best value on this page, as long as you remember that "per location" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.
